# 04 · Product

## The concept

> Scan a wall once. Every visit after that, point a camera at it and the route appears on the real holds.

The everyday path has three steps: **pick a wall → choose or generate a route → climb**. Scanning is the exception, done once per wall version by whoever gets there first.

## The fundamental decisions

These were worked out on day one and are the backbone of everything else.

**1. Walls have versions.** Gyms reset walls every 6–8 weeks. Re-scanning a wall creates version N+1; version N, its photo and its routes are archived, never deleted. Nothing points at the wrong hold, and climbers keep their history. The product works best on walls that rarely change — spray walls, system boards, home walls, real rock — which is exactly where all three personas climb.

**2. Scan once, then just point.** "Scan every session" and "pick from existing walls" are not opposites: the live camera locking onto the stored photo *is* the scan, automatically, every time. Tapping reference points happens only when adding a wall or a new version. Anyone can add a wall; there is no owner role.

**3. The app writes the first routes.** A wall with zero routes loses its first user. Once hold positions are known, routes can be generated by rules — so a wall is never empty, and generation answers Lena directly ("give me a sloper route").

**4. Three moments, and the phone is useless in one of them.** Before the climb: choose, see the route on the wall, memorise it. During: nothing — hands are on holds. After: review. Anything that needs attention mid-climb (the smartwatch idea) fails; audio is the only channel that works during a climb.

**5. Never guess.** If the wall can't be matched, draw nothing and say which way to move. A wrong overlay destroys trust instantly.

**6. Real centimetres.** Reach guardrails need scale; a photo only gives pixels. The scan asks for one real measurement.

**7. Rules, not AI, for route generation.** Every generated move is guaranteed within reach and explainable, with no training data. AI belongs on the roadmap — learning from which routes people complete and like.

**8. Own the data.** After the Kilter–Aurora split, ArtRock should own the route database and wall maps, even if a partner builds the app.

**9. Who pays (proposal).** Free for climbers, because they are the content. Gyms pay monthly per wall; therapists per practice; or ArtRock bundles the app with walls it sells, so the software sells walls.

**10. Physical products are a moat.** Ideas only a manufacturer can execute: a wall-mounted phone cradle at the right height and angle; holds with a moulded identifier, so position and hold type come free with a scan; the hold map shipped with every new wall.

## The three working groups

The team of nine split the features into three groups. Each had to answer "how does this work in the app?" and produce 3–4 screens.

### Group 1 · Wall and finding boulders

Features: scan the wall (photo + 3–4 reference points) · route shown in the camera view · hold categories (jug, pinch, crimp, sloper) · routes by difficulty · filters (holds, angle, grade) · map with GPS (gyms and spray walls nearby) · safety guidelines.

Questions: how is the wall scanned step by step · how are holds tagged and a boulder created without AI · who sets the grade (creator or community vote) · which filters do Lena, Max and Laura need · where do safety guidelines appear.

Screens asked for: scan screen · create a boulder · boulder list with filters · route in the camera view. Story: "From empty wall to first boulder".

**Emilio owns the first two features (scan, overlay)** — designed in 05-design.md and built in `ascent.py`, together with generation, filters and hold types.

### Group 2 · Training and progress

Features: climbing history / logbook · statistics · milestones and badges · training recommendations without AI · competition mode · smartwatch connection (e.g. Garmin).

Questions: how is a boulder logged (sent / tried / fail) · which numbers matter (count, grade, hold types, training time) · how do recommendations work without AI (rules like "you rarely climb pinches → 3 pinch boulders", coach plans) · what does progress look like for Max (levels, badges), Laura (rehab goals) and Lena (skills) · how do you challenge someone and what do you win.

Screens asked for: log a boulder · progress dashboard (stats + milestones) · training recommendation · send a challenge. Story: "A week with Lena".

Notes from the project: the smartwatch is not in ArtRock's brief, and looking at a watch mid-climb is impossible. Rewards for minors must not favour difficulty or streaks.

### Group 3 · Community and app basics

Features: share and connect (boulders, achievements) · video library (upload, share beta, fail videos) · chat · blog · team sessions (train together) · feedback area · sign-up, languages (DE/EN), data security.

Questions: how do people find each other (friends, same gym, same goals such as shoulder rehab) · how is a video linked to a boulder, how do you ask for beta · how is a team session started at the spray wall · what does sign-up ask (goal Play / Rehab / Train, language, level) · who sees which videos and data (private / friends / public — important for children and health data).

Screens asked for: sign-up + choose goal · community feed · boulder page with videos and beta questions · team session / chat. Story: "Max downloads the app and climbs with friends".

Notes from the project: of these, ArtRock's brief only asks for accounts and a shared route database. Blog, chat, video library and team sessions came from the team's own ideas.

### Not assigned to any group

**Motion tracking with audio feedback** — the concept paper's "premier discipline" — was not owned by any group during the event. The entry point exists ("Record my try" on the overlay screen) and the test rig includes a wooden mannequin for exactly this. Given ClimbAI, the recommended scope is minimal: detect which hold a hand is on, and play a sound for right or wrong order.

## The 22 ideas from the brainstorm

The team's sticky-note board, triaged for the hackathon:

- **Built for real:** holds recognition (positions on one wall) · route on the live camera · routes categorised by difficulty
- **Designed or mocked:** filters · hold categories · climbing history · statistics · milestones · sign-up · training recommendations · video library · community sharing · competition mode
- **Roadmap only:** map with GPS · languages · blog and chat · team sessions · security · safety guidelines · sponsor rewards · speech model · smartwatch · AI training recommendations

## Feature scope used for the prototype

| | Feature | Serves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Must | Photograph the wall, mark holds, define routes, live overlay | everyone |
| Must | Tag routes by what they train | Lena, Laura |
| Should | Save and browse routes per wall | everyone |
| Should | "N people climbed this" | Lena, Max |
| Should | Anyone can create and publish routes | Max, Laura's therapist |
| Could | Record an attempt, basic hold contact | Lena, Laura |
| Won't (now) | Full movement analysis (partner) · leaderboards · smart glasses | — |

## Roadmap

- **Now (hackathon):** laptop prototype on the test panel — scan, generate, build, live overlay; clickable design of the phone app.
- **Phase 1:** phone app for Lena's journey; accounts and a shared route database; duplicate walls merged by gym and location.
- **Phase 2 — Max:** creating and sharing routes outdoors, offline mode, youth-safe gamification, parental consent.
- **Phase 2 — Laura:** therapist dashboard, prescribed routes, session documentation.
- **Phase 3:** movement feedback through a partner (e.g. ClimbAI), audio guidance, smart glasses, learning-based route recommendations, detecting partial resets, moulded hold identifiers.
