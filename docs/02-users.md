# 02 · Users

The team built three personas. The product is designed for **Lena first**; Max and Laura shape phase two. The persona canvas follows the DT-Toolbox template (description, job to be done, use cases, pains, gains, sketch, influencers, trends).

## Lena — the primary persona

**Lena Hofer, 28, Innsbruck.** Office job. Boulders twice a week at a small local gym, three years in, around grade 6a. No coach, doesn't compete. Hikes, ski tours in winter. Climbs with one friend, often alone.

> "I know what I want to get better at. I don't know what to climb to get there."

- **Job to be done:** turn a wall of random holds into a session that targets her weakness, without a coach.
- **Weak at:** slopers, overhangs, footwork when tired — and she knows it.
- **Pains:** the gym resets every 6–8 weeks and she clears her grade in two; the spray wall is 300 holds with no structure, so she repeats the same three moves; she can't tell whether a route she invents trains the right thing; nobody gives her feedback; she can't tell if she's improving; she already films herself with her phone propped on her bag and learns nothing from it.
- **Gains:** always something new at her level; routes labelled by the skill they train; visible progress; feeling competent at the spray wall; free; shareable with her gym.
- **Quits if:** setup is slow, it needs a laptop or a partner, or feedback is generic.
- **Influencers:** her climbing friend, stronger climbers at the gym, the route setter, Instagram and YouTube.
- **Demands:** routes tagged **by skill, not only by grade**. No competitor does this.

## Max — the young outdoor climber

**Max, 14,** lives in a village in Tirol with a free outdoor boulder area. No gym, no coach, no money of his own. Videogames, VR, adventures with friends. Self-taught for about two years.

- **Job to be done:** make climbing alone feel like it counts — something he can show his friends.
- **Pains:** his friends don't climb, so he goes alone and gets bored; same four boulders; no idea if he's improving; nothing to show for it.
- **Gains:** a number that goes up; status from a route he set that others climb; a reason to go out; real rewards.
- **Team ideas:** leaderboards (asynchronous, so friends don't need to be present), challenges with rewards, "number of people who climbed this route", safety guidelines.
- **Key insights from the critique:**
  - Max is a **creator**, not just a player — setting routes on undocumented boulders is the growth engine.
  - **Who pays** for a 14-year-old: parents, a club, the Alpenverein.
  - **Filming a minor** needs parental consent and careful data handling.
  - **Streaks and difficulty leaderboards push a child to climb harder, alone, on rock, with no spotter.** Reward consistency, routes created, routes repeated by others, technique — never hardest grade.
  - Outdoors: no signal (offline mode), no discrete holds, access and nature-protection rules.

## Laura — the rehab persona

**Laura, 32, artist.** Bike and motorbike, Tour de France, skiing, cooking. Shoulder surgery two years ago, lost fine motor skill. Climbs in a rehab clinic, gyms and climbing centres.

- **Gains wanted:** regain function; support and community during recovery; avoid overload; complex compound movement.
- **Pains:** long, painful recovery; repetitive, boring exercises; overworking.
- **Influencers:** social media, reviews from experience.
- **Key insights:**
  - Two users in one: **the therapist sets the route, the patient climbs it.** The therapist is the buyer.
  - Climbing therapy (*Therapieklettern*) is established in Austria and Germany — a market with budgets.
  - Position the app as **a tool the therapist uses**, never as something that judges whether a movement is safe for the patient — that would drift towards a medical device.
  - Rehab data is health data under GDPR: the strictest category.

## The convergence

All three need the same core feature:

> **A route is a set of holds, tagged by what it trains, drawn onto the live view of the wall.**

Lena tags by skill, Max by fun and difficulty (and creates routes), Laura's therapist by movement. One mechanism, three markets.

## Segments: who uses it, who pays

| Segment | Pain | Who pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home-wall owners | Few holds, boring fast, LED boards too expensive | The owner |
| Gyms with spray walls | Spray walls underused, resetting is physical work | The gym, monthly |
| Route setters | Hours per reset, no digital record | Their employer |
| Therapists | Need a specific movement performed and documented | Practice, possibly insurance |
| Children, schools, clubs | Kids freeze in front of 200 holds | School or club |
| Events and parties | Non-climbers need instant engagement | Venue |
| Training climbers | Technique feedback | Themselves — **already served by ClimbAI** |
| Outdoor climbers | Sharing a line on real rock | Nobody yet — the network effect |
| ArtRock | Differentiate against LED competitors | Internal |

**Beachhead:** regular climbers at small gyms and home walls (Lena). **Second phase:** therapists — the one segment with a clear budget and a reason to pay.

A free app is distribution, not charity: free users build the route database, and the paying side is people who need management, documentation or many walls.
